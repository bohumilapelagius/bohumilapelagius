## Overview of the Investigation

Republika Srpska is one of Bosnia and Herzegovina’s two administrative entities, situated directly on the external border of the European Union. Decisions regarding its critical information infrastructure impact public institutions, citizens' private data, regional telecommunications, and cross-border financial channels.

Appointed in February 2024 to lead the newly established **Agency for Information and Communication Technologies (AIKT)**, **Dražen Višnjić** brought a background spanning military academy training in Belgrade, police telecommunications interception, international cybercrime cooperation, corporate directorships, and senior political advisory roles under Milorad Dodik. Rather than establishing transparent, auditable European cybersecurity standards, AIKT under Višnjić’s leadership became the operational hub for a series of opaque contracts and geopolitical alignments.

## Key Findings & Documented Evidence

### 1. The BAM 57 Million (USD 31M) Secret Deal with Sanctioned Chinese Defense Contractor ELINC
Following U.S. Treasury sanctions that crippled Milorad Dodik’s domestic IT patronage network and triggered the December 2023 collapse of Republika Srpska’s Integrated Health Information System (IZIS) alongside dark-web leaks of Tax Administration records, the entity government pivoted to Beijing.
* **The Contract:** In May 2024, authorities quietly signed a 10-year, **BAM 57 million** software and infrastructure contract with **ELINC** (*China Electronics International Corporation*), a subsidiary of state-owned defense conglomerate **China Electronics Corporation (CEC)**.
* **Defense & Surveillance Profile:** CEC was placed on the U.S. Treasury’s Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) *Non-SDN Chinese Military-Industrial Complex Companies List* under an executive order targeting surveillance technology exported outside China to monitor citizens and facilitate repression. ELINC previously debuted in the Balkans at the 2023 Belgrade arms fair, showcasing radar-jamming and electronic-warfare systems.
* **The Tripartite Model:** Višnjić publicly defended the rollout across 15 core locations and 780 public institutions (with data centers at the Faculty of Electrical Engineering in East Sarajevo and backup support in Kragujevac, Serbia). He explicitly defined the architecture as a combination of **Chinese hardware and software, Russian organizational mechanisms of "control and supervision," and Israeli technical training**.
* **Absence of Oversight:** When questioned by journalists on what independent mechanism would prevent political abuse or unlawful surveillance, Višnjić admitted that an oversight model was merely "under consideration"—months after public funds were already committed.

### 2. Official Alignment with Sanctioned Russian Propaganda Network Rybar
Despite AIKT’s legal mandate to protect information security and public data integrity, the agency actively courted Kremlin-linked information-warfare operatives.
* **The Agreement:** On June 17, 2024, Russia’s **Rybar** network announced that its head, **Mikhail Zvinchuk**, and AIKT director Dražen Višnjić had agreed on bilateral cooperation in the Balkans.
* **Institutional Confirmation:** In July 2024, AIKT officially confirmed to investigative reporters at *Detektor* (BIRN) that it planned formal cooperation with Zvinchuk, including establishing a "media school" and joint academic initiatives with local faculties.
* **Sanctions Context:** Zvinchuk and Rybar are sanctioned by the European Union for undermining the territorial integrity and sovereignty of Ukraine and operate as a primary Kremlin influence instrument funded via Russian state-military channels.

### 3. Sanctions-Evasion & Cryptocurrency Settlement Questionnaire
In August 2025, investigative outlet *CAPITAL.ba* exposed an official questionnaire circulated to public institutions directly from an institutional AIKT email address (`@aikt.vladars.rs`), ostensibly mapping IT cooperation with Russia’s Nizhny Novgorod region.
* **Financial Workarounds:** Beyond routine fiber-optic and data-center questions, the document systematically probed Republika Srpska’s legal and financial capacity to facilitate **sanctions evasion** for Russian tech companies.
* **Alternative Channels:** Specific inquiries targeted alternative banking settlement channels via **China and the United Arab Emirates (UAE)**, intermediary re-export routes into the European Union via **Serbia and Turkey**, and institutional payment mechanisms utilizing **cryptocurrencies and stablecoins**.
* **Unaccounted Infrastructure:** While AIKT publicly denied authoring the document and Investment-Development Bank (IRB RS) leadership later claimed association with the Russian outreach, AIKT never released server logs, email headers, or an internal incident audit explaining how an official cybersecurity agency inbox distributed a Russian sanctions-evasion blueprint.

### 4. The 12-Minute, BAM 1 Million Media Procurement Ambush
On June 7, 2024, just weeks after the secret Chinese contract was signed, AIKT launched a **BAM 1 million (excluding VAT)** framework tender for a 12-month media advertising campaign.
* **Procedural Manipulation:** Procurement watchdog *Pratimo Tendere* and investigative portal *Gerila* discovered that the million-mark item did not exist in AIKT’s original procurement schedule. The budget amendment was inserted into the public procurement plan **just 12 minutes** before the tender notice was published.
* **Tailored Specifications:** Bidders were required to maintain television studio facilities across three specific cities (Banja Luka, East Sarajevo, and Trebinje), deploy five filming crews, five editing suites, and drone equipment, and prove prior contracts worth at least BAM 1 million—criteria matching the exact operational footprint of broadcasters affiliated with *Alternativna Televizija (ATV)*, a media outlet sanctioned by the U.S. Treasury in January 2022 as part of Milorad Dodik’s corruption network.

### 5. Police Wiretapping, Unreported Trips to Russia, and a BAM 6M Pharmaceutical Scheme
Višnjić’s appointment to AIKT followed a documented 15-year pattern inside Republika Srpska’s security and political apparatus:
* **Interior Ministry Wiretapping Operations (2011):** Entering the RS Ministry of Interior (MUP RS) around 2008 after graduating from the Belgrade Military Academy and serving in the Armed Forces of BiH, Višnjić was placed at the controls of BAM 4 million in surveillance and interception hardware acquired from Belgrade-based *Vizus* (largely funded through Dodik’s presidential budget). Investigative reporting by Slobodan Vasković documented how the system extracted call records directly from the *m:tel* network without judicial warrants, and how Višnjić—protected by presidential security adviser Miloš Čubrilović and relative Zoran Mandić—expanded interception playback access across more computers than licensed. When unit supervisor Zlatan Mitić objected, Mitić was removed and Višnjić was promoted.
* **FBI Commendation vs. Internal Criminal Inquiry (2016–2017):** In May 2016, Višnjić and five colleagues in the MUP RS high-tech crime unit received an FBI commendation for the international "Port Royal" cybercrime operation. However, by October 2017, after Višnjić and four subordinates abruptly resigned, the MUP RS Organized Crime Directorate launched a prosecutorial inquiry into the unit. Investigators examined the handling of FBI-donated hardware (10 iPhones, 4 iPads, 7 laptops) and a USD 30,000 server payment, alongside allegations that data from court-warranted undercover online surveillance had been illegally copied onto external hard drives outside the judicial chain of custody. State Prosecutor Diana Kajmaković also filed an official dispatch noting that Višnjić’s unit had shared investigative data with German police without her authorization. Crucially, the complaint targeted Višnjić’s **unreported trips to Russia and Switzerland in 2015 and 2016**. The inquiry vanished into prosecutorial silence as Višnjić moved directly into Milorad Dodik’s office as a presidential adviser.
* **The BAM 6 Million "Dulin" Sepsis Patent (2020):** In November 2020, *BN Television* exposed a scheme in which the RS Ministry of Health and the Investment-Development Bank (IRB RS) wired **BAM 6 million** in three tranches to the private account of San Diego physician Dušan Ninkov for a 25% stake in "Dulin," a purported sepsis treatment never tested on humans. The corporate vehicle registered in Banja Luka, *Tesla Bioscience a.d.*, was co-owned by the RS Government, Ninkov, and Belgrade-based *Iris Institut*—whose director was **Dražen Višnjić** and whose owner was **Dragan Vasiljević** (a notorious Dodik associate linked to the collapse of the *Farmland* cattle farm and the looting of over BAM 18 million from IRB RS and the entity’s Commodity Reserves). To date, no working medication or financial return has ever been accounted for to taxpayers.
